The Banagher Leisurely Tour

Four nights on the Lower Shannon. North through the Callows to Athlone and Lough Ree, back down past Clonmacnoise. Around nine engine hours across the whole trip, no locks, and never far from a pub.

Passage plan

Built around Silverline's actual terms: boarding from 4pm on day one, boat back before 10am on the final morning. That's why the last night is Shannonbridge, not Clonmacnoise — it leaves a relaxed 80-minute run home instead of a 7:30am scramble.

Fuel is charged per engine hour (~€15–16/h on Shannon hire fleets) → budget ≈ €140–160 against the fuel deposit. Longest cruising day is 3½ hours; most are under two. If the Clonmacnoise-overnight romance appeals, it works — but it means casting off by 7:45am on day 5. Shannonbridge is the grown-up call.

Lough Ree gate — goWind Force 4 or below and no small-craft warning: Day 3 runs as planned. Cross in the morning; the lake is usually calmest before noon.
Lough Ree gate — no goAbove Force 4, don't take a hire boat onto Lough Ree. Plan B: stay in Athlone a second night — castle, Viking ship, greenway cycle — and taxi to Baysports (15 min) if the park itself is running. The trip loses nothing important.

Banagher Harbour
⚓ Start & finish · Night 1 aboard at the marina
A proper river town with a seven-arch bridge, a Napoleonic fort, and one of the best trad pubs on the Shannon. Everything is within a five-minute walk of the marina.
▾
Base logistics: Silverline will meet you at the marina; instruction and demo included, no licence needed. Suitcases can be stored at the marina — pack soft bags for the boat. They'll also pre-stock the boat with essentials if you order ahead, and bikes can be hired and loaded on arrival. The supermarket is a five-minute walk.
Do this at handoverPhoto and video the hull, prop area, fenders and interior, and get the condition sheet signed — deposit disputes are won here. Confirm what the damage waiver excludes (grounding recovery and prop damage are the usual carve-outs), and whether the deposit is a pre-auth or a charge.

250-year-old heritage pub, ivy-covered front, trad music most nights in season. Part of the floor is reclaimed from a 14th-century monastery. Ask for a Bo Bristle — brewed a few doors up.

Shannonbridge
⛵ ~1h 20 from Banagher
A one-street village either side of a beautiful sixteen-arch bridge, with a Napoleonic fort guarding the crossing. A lunch stop today — you're back here for the last night.
▾
Mooring: Jetties on both sides of the bridge, usually space. The bridge arches are narrow — take it slow and pick your arch early.
🍽️ Eat & drink
Luker's Old Bar & Restaurant
The reason to stop. Right on the water, famous locally for sunsets over the Shannon and trad sessions. Proper pub food done well — lunch today, dinner on night 4.

Athlone
⛵ ~2h 10 from Shannonbridge · Night 2 on the quay
The big stop. A Norman castle looming over the quay, the oldest pub in Ireland a minute's walk from your mooring, and enough restaurants to make choosing hard.
▾
Mooring: Town centre quay below the castle — the best mooring on the route, straight into the middle of everything. Fills up in season and there's a small nightly harbour charge; aim to arrive by mid-afternoon.
🍽️ Eat & drink
Sean's Bar
Guinness World Record holder as the oldest pub in Ireland, with wattle-and-daub walls found during 1970s renovations to back the claim. Sloping sawdust floor, own-brand whiskey, trad sessions. It will be rammed — go early afternoon or late.

Hodson Bay
⛵ ~50 min from Athlone
Where the river opens out into Lough Ree and the horizon suddenly gets a lot wider. Also where the single biggest kid-pleaser on the whole trip lives.
▾
Mooring: Jetty by the hotel and Baysports on the western shore. More exposed than the river moorings — check which way the wind is blowing before you tie up for long.
🎯 Activities
Baysports
Ireland's largest inflatable waterpark, floating on Lough Ree, including the Guinness-record tallest inflatable slide in the world. Wetsuits and lifejackets provided, lifeguarded throughout, and a separate junior park for ages two to six so Caoimhe-sized crew aren't left on the bank. Kayak/waterpark combos around €39. Sessions are limited on quieter weekdays — book online before you head north.

6
Killinure Lough
⛵ ~40 min from Hodson Bay · Turnaround · Night 3 at anchor
A sheltered inner lough tucked off the eastern side of Lough Ree. No village, no pub, no facilities — that's the entire point. The quietest night of the trip.
▾
Mooring: An anchorage, not a marina. Sheltered from most wind directions, which is what makes it usable when the main lough is lively. If the forecast turns overnight, drop back to Athlone instead — no shame in it. Run the engine before evening to top up the batteries; on the 12V boats this is your only power.
🌿 Outdoors & nature
Birdwatching
Herons, swans, cormorants, great crested grebes. Still water and no engine noise, so you actually see things. Best at dusk and first thing.
🔭 BinocularsAll ages
Fishing
Pike, perch and bream. Buy or hire gear in Athlone on the way through — there's nothing to buy out here.
🎣 Gear from Athlone
Swimming
Calm and sheltered, swimmable in settled weather. Lifejackets on, adult in the water, engine off.
Supervised

Whatever's in the fridge
Nothing here. Cook aboard — this is the night for it. Provision in Athlone before heading north.

Clonmacnoise
⛵ ~2h 15 from Killinure via Athlone · The big afternoon stop
A sixth-century monastic city on the riverbank — round towers, high crosses, nine churches and seven hundred grave slabs. Arriving by water is how it was meant to be seen, and almost nobody does it that way any more.
▾
Mooring: River jetty below the site — moor up and walk straight in. Aim to arrive after 3pm when the coach crowds thin; last admission 5:30pm in summer. Then it's a gentle 40 minutes on to Shannonbridge for the night. (The overnight-at-the-jetty alternative is magical but forces a 7:45am start on day 5 — your call.)
2026 — changed since springThe visitor centre is closed for a €7.8m redevelopment: no audiovisual show, and the original high crosses are off display. The monastic site itself is fully open with temporary toilets and a welcome hub. Small groups are walk-in only — no online booking, no guided tours this year. Download the OPW visitor guide PDF before you go; there's no guide on the day.
🏰 History & heritage
The monastic site
Founded by St Ciarán in the sixth century at the crossing of the Shannon and the Esker Riada, and for four hundred years the closest thing Ireland had to a university. A cathedral, two round towers, replica high crosses, nine churches, and the burial place of two High Kings. Allow ninety minutes to two hours.
Best on the route€8 / €4 child / €20 familyLast adm. 5:30pm
The Nuns' Church
Three hundred metres east of the main enclosure along a green lane. One of the finest Hiberno-Romanesque doorways in the country, usually completely deserted.
🚶 5 minFree
👧 With kids
How it actually plays
Better than you'd expect. The ruins are open and explorable, there's grass to run on, and the round towers do the work. With the AV show out of action, give them a hunt — count the high crosses, find the carved animals on the grave slabs, spot the swifts nesting in the tower.

Night 4 · Shannonbridge

Forty minutes downstream from Clonmacnoise, moor by the bridge, and take the table at Luker's you scouted on Day 2 — this is the sunset-over-the-Shannon dinner. You're now 80 minutes from base with the hard cruising done, which is exactly where you want to be on the last night.

05
Shannonbridge → Banagher
~1h 20 engine time · Boat back before 10am
Last leg

Lines off by 8:15, coffee at the upper helm, and a final run down through the Callows to be alongside in Banagher by 9:40 — inside Silverline's 10am return with margin. Do the return walkaround with the same camera you used at handover, hand back the keys, collect the suitcases from the marina office.

If legs need stretching before the drive home: Birr Castle is fifteen minutes away and earns a half day — treehouse for the kids, telescope for you.

On the water

Practical notes

  • 🌬️
    Lough Ree is the one to watchIt builds a short, steep chop very quickly and it's the only genuinely exposed water on this route. Use the wind gate above: F4 or below, go; above that, Plan B. Cross in the morning when the lake is calmest, and check the forecast at the quay each day, not the night before.
  • 🔒
    No locks on this routeBanagher up to Lough Ree is lock-free. Meelick, the nearest, is downstream toward Portumna — you won't meet it unless you head south.
  • ⛽
    Fuel, water & powerFill both at Banagher before departure; Athlone is the mid-trip top-up. Some Silverline boats run 12V battery systems — run the engine daily to charge, use power sparingly at anchor, and note USB may be the only socket. Shore power in Athlone if your boat takes it.
  • 🛒
    ProvisioningTwo real shops on the route: Banagher and Athlone. Shannonbridge has a village shop for milk and ice cream. Stock properly in Athlone on Day 4 before the last two hops.
  • 🧭
    NavigationWell-marked with red and green channel markers — keep red to starboard heading upstream. Five knots through the towns, wake discipline past moored boats. Carry the Waterways Ireland Shannon chart; this page is not a navigation aid.
  • 📶
    SignalFine in the towns and along most of the river. Patchy on Lough Ree and around Killinure — this page works offline once loaded, but download maps, the Clonmacnoise guide PDF, and anything for the kids before heading north.
  • 🛟
    Kids aboardLifejackets on deck and on jetties, always. Engine off before anyone swims. Brief both on where to sit under way and where not to stand during mooring — the person on ropes needs clear space.
  • 📋
    Deposit protectionPhoto/video walkthrough at handover and return, signed condition sheet both times. Know the waiver carve-outs (grounding recovery, prop damage, lost keys are typical). Check your travel policy for hire-boat excess cover — many exclude watercraft.
