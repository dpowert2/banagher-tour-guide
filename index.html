The Banagher Leisurely Tour

Four nights on the Lower Shannon. North through the Callows to Athlone and Lough Ree, back down past Clonmacnoise. Roughly four hours' cruising a day, and never far from a pub.

Banagher Harbour
⚓ Start & finish · Co. Offaly
A proper river town with a seven-arch bridge, a Napoleonic fort, and one of the best trad pubs on the Shannon. Everything is within a five-minute walk of the marina.
▾
Mooring: Silver Star's marina sits right at the town end of the harbour. Pontoon berths, power, water and fuel on site. Town centre is a two-minute walk up the hill.
Do this firstPhoto and video the hull, prop area, fenders and interior at handover, and get the condition sheet signed. Deposit disputes are won here. Top up fuel and water before you leave.

Shannonbridge
⛵ ~1 hour from Banagher
A one-street village either side of a beautiful sixteen-arch bridge, with a Napoleonic fort guarding the crossing. Thirty minutes will do it — unless you're eating at Luker's, in which case give it two.
▾
Mooring: Jetties on both sides of the bridge, usually space. The bridge arches are narrow — take it slow and pick your arch early.
🍽️ Eat & drink
Luker's Old Bar & Restaurant
The reason to stop. Right on the water, famous locally for sunsets over the Shannon and for trad sessions. Food is proper pub food done well.
Must do🚶 1 minFamily friendly
🏰 History & heritage
The sixteen-arch bridge
One of the oldest crossings on the Shannon and genuinely striking from the water. You'll go under it — have the camera ready on the approach.
⛵ From the boatFree
Napoleonic fortifications
A full defensive complex — batteries, redoubt, bridgehead — built to stop a French army crossing here. Self-guided walk around the whole thing, no ticket, no queue.
🚶 5 minGood for kidsFree

Bog walks
The raised bog around Shannonbridge is a strange, flat, enormous landscape unlike anywhere else on the route. Easy flat walking straight out of the village.

Athlone
⛵ ~2 hours from Shannonbridge (past Clonmacnoise)
The big stop. A Norman castle looming over the quay, the oldest pub in Ireland a minute's walk from your mooring, and enough restaurants to make choosing hard. Two nights here would not be wasted.
▾
Mooring: Town centre quay below the castle — the best mooring on the route, and you step off into the middle of everything. Fills up in season, so aim to arrive by mid-afternoon.
🍽️ Eat & drink
Sean's Bar
Guinness World Record holder as the oldest pub in Ireland, with wattle-and-daub walls found during 1970s renovations to back the claim. Sloping sawdust floor, own-brand whiskey, trad sessions. It will be rammed — go early afternoon or late.
Must do🚶 1 minGo early with kids
Thyme Restaurant
Riverside, modern Irish, the best dinner in town. Book ahead — it's small and it fills.

Hodson Bay
⛵ ~45 min from Athlone
Where the river opens out into Lough Ree and the horizon suddenly gets a lot wider. Also where the single biggest kid-pleaser on the whole trip lives.
▾
Mooring: Jetty by the hotel and Baysports on the western shore. More exposed than the river moorings — check which way the wind is blowing before you tie up for long.
🎯 Activities
Baysports
Ireland's largest inflatable waterpark, floating on Lough Ree, including the Guinness-record tallest inflatable slide in the world. Wetsuits and lifejackets provided, lifeguarded throughout. There's a separate junior park for ages two to six, so the small one isn't left on the bank. Kayaks, paddleboards and pedal boats too — combo tickets around €39.
Trip highlightGreat for kids🚶 On the doorstepBook online first

Pay-per-use leisure centre for non-residents. The wet-weather backup if the lake is too rough for Baysports.

Killinure Lough
⛵ ~40 min from Hodson Bay · Turnaround
A sheltered inner lough tucked off the eastern side of Lough Ree. No village, no pub, no facilities — that's the entire point. The quietest night of the trip.
▾
Mooring: This is an anchorage, not a marina. Sheltered from most wind directions, which is what makes it usable when the main lough is lively. If the forecast turns, drop back to Athlone for the night instead — no shame in it.
🌿 Outdoors & nature
Birdwatching
Herons, swans, cormorants, great crested grebes. Still water and no engine noise, so you actually see things. Best at dusk and first thing.
🔭 BinocularsAll ages
Fishing
Pike, perch and bream. Buy or hire gear in Athlone on the way through if anyone fancies it — there's nothing to buy out here.
🎣 Gear from Athlone
Swimming
Calm and sheltered, and swimmable in settled weather. Lifejackets on, adult in the water, boat engine off.
Supervised
🍽️ Eat & drink
Whatever's in the fridge
Nothing here. Cook aboard — this is the night for it. Make sure you've provisioned in Athlone before you head north.

Clonmacnoise
⛵ ~2h 45 from Killinure via Athlone
A sixth-century monastic city on the riverbank — round towers, high crosses, nine churches and seven hundred grave slabs. Arriving by water is how it was meant to be seen, and almost nobody does it that way any more.
▾
Mooring: There's a river jetty below the site — moor up and walk straight in. Overnight is peaceful; you'll have the ruins to yourselves once the coaches leave around five.
2026 — check before you goThe visitor centre is closed for a €7.8m redevelopment, so there's no audiovisual presentation and the original high crosses aren't on display. The monastic site itself stays fully open with temporary toilets and a welcome hub. Small groups are walk-in only — no online booking, no guided tours. Bring a guidebook or download the OPW visitor guide, because you won't get a guide on the day.
🏰 History & heritage
The monastic site
Founded by St Ciarán in the sixth century at the crossing of the Shannon and the Esker Riada, and for four hundred years the closest thing Ireland had to a university. A cathedral, two round towers, replica high crosses, nine churches, and the burial place of two High Kings. Allow an hour and a half to two hours.
Best on the route€8 adult · €4 child · €20 familyDaily 9:00–17:30
The Nuns' Church
Three hundred metres east of the main enclosure along a green lane, and worth every step. One of the finest Hiberno-Romanesque doorways in the country, and usually completely deserted.
🚶 5 minFree
🌿 Outdoors & nature
Riverside walk from the jetty
Flat walking along the Callows with the ruins on the skyline. Good light in the evening and the best photograph you'll take all week.
🚶 From the jettyAll ages
👧 With kids
How it actually plays
Better than you'd expect. The ruins are open and explorable, there's grass to run on, and the round towers do the work. With the AV presentation out of action this year, give them something to hunt for — count the high crosses, find the carved animals on the grave slabs.

Clonmacnoise → Banagher
~2 hours cruising · Home by lunchtime, boat back, pint earned
Last leg

Two hours back downstream through the Callows, under the sixteen arches at Shannonbridge, and into Banagher. Aim to be alongside by late morning if handover is at noon — leave slack for fuelling, and do the return walkaround with the same camera you used on day one.

If you've time before the drive home: JJ Hough's for one, or Birr Castle if you skipped it at the start.

On the water

Practical notes

  • 🌬️
    Lough Ree is the one to watchIt builds a short, steep chop very quickly and it's the only genuinely exposed water on this route. Above Force 4, stay in the river below Athlone — the trip works perfectly well without going north. Check the forecast at the quay each morning, not the night before.
  • 🔒
    No locks on this stretchThe route from Banagher up to Lough Ree is lock-free. Meelick, the nearest one, is downstream of Banagher toward Portumna — you won't meet it unless you head south.
  • ⛽
    Fuel and waterFill both at Banagher before departure. Athlone is your reliable mid-trip top-up. Don't count on anything at Killinure.
  • 🛒
    ProvisioningTwo real shops on the route: Banagher and Athlone. Shannonbridge has a village shop for milk and ice cream, and that's it. Stock properly in Athlone before turning north.
  • 🧭
    NavigationWell-marked with red and green channel markers — keep red to starboard heading upstream. Five knots through the towns, wake discipline past moored boats. Carry the Waterways Ireland Shannon chart; this page is not a navigation aid.
  • 📶
    SignalFine in the towns and along most of the river. Patchy out on Lough Ree and around Killinure — download anything you need before heading north.
  • 🛟
    Kids aboardLifejackets on deck and on jetties, always. Engine off before anyone swims. Brief both of them on where to sit while under way and where not to stand during mooring.
