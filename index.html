What it means for this trip: the bow thruster takes the drama out of the Athlone quay in a crosswind; the bathing platform with steps is the Killinure swim sorted; central heating covers a cold snap; and with proper 220V aboard you're not rationing power like the 12V boats — though still give the engine a run at anchor to keep the batteries honest.

The other option from Banagher

North or south?

Banagher sits mid-river, so both directions are open. Silverline's 4-night published route actually goes south — Lough Derg, Terryglass, Dromineer, Garrykennedy, Killaloe. Here's the honest comparison for this crew and this clock:

North (this plan)South (Lough Derg)
Locks & bridgesNoneMeelick Lock + Portumna swing bridge — the bridge only opens at scheduled times, so both directions of your trip are pinned to a timetable
Open waterSouthern Lough Ree only, with a same-day bail-out to AthloneLough Derg is bigger, with longer exposed crossings between villages and fewer bolt-holes
For the kidsBaysports, Viking ship, castle museum, greenway bikes, river poolPortumna forest park & castle, lovely village greens — charming, thinner on headline acts
Villages & pubsGood (Shannonbridge, Athlone's density)Better — Terryglass and Garrykennedy are the prettiest harbours on the Shannon
Fit to 4 nights + 10am returnComfortable: ~9¼ engine hours, no timetablesTight: lock + bridge windows twice, longer legs, less slack for weather

Verdict: north, clearly — for this trip. With Oisín and Caoimhe aboard, four nights, and a hard 10am return, north gives more to do ashore, no timetabled infrastructure, and better escape routes when the wind gets up. South is the better adult cruise and the right second trip: give Lough Derg six or seven nights and it out-prettifies everything north of Meelick.

▾

A one-street village either side of a beautiful sixteen-arch bridge, with a Napoleonic fort guarding the crossing. Arrive ~6:30pm with hours of light left, moor, and walk one minute to dinner. You'll pass under the arches again at dawn on Day 5.

Clonmacnoise
⛵ ~2h 15 from Killinure via Athlone · Night 4 at the jetty
🏰 Heritage🥾 Riverside walk
A sixth-century monastic city on the riverbank — round towers, high crosses, nine churches, seven hundred grave slabs. Arriving by water is how it was meant to be seen — and overnighting below the ruins means you get them at dusk with everyone else gone.
▾
Mooring: River jetty below the site — moor up and walk straight in. Arrive after 3pm when the coach crowds thin; last admission 5:30pm in summer. Then stay put: once the car park barrier drops around 6, the towers, the Callows and the sunset are yours. The trade is a 7:45am start on Day 5 — if you'd rather a lazier last morning, push on 40 minutes to Shannonbridge tonight and leave at 8:15 instead.
2026 — changed since springThe visitor centre is closed for a €7.8m redevelopment: no audiovisual show, and the original high crosses are off display. The monastic site itself is fully open with temporary toilets and a welcome hub. Small groups are walk-in only — no online booking, no guided tours this year. 🏰 History & heritage
The monastic site
Founded by St Ciarán in the sixth century at the crossing of the Shannon and the Esker Riada — for four hundred years the closest thing Ireland had to a university. A cathedral, two round towers, replica high crosses, nine churches, and the burial place of two High Kings. Allow ninety minutes to two hours.

On the water

Practical notes

  • 🌬️
    Lough Ree is the one to watchIt builds a short, steep chop very quickly and it's the only genuinely exposed water on this route. Use the live wind gate above: F4 or below, go; above that, Plan B. Check at the quay each morning, not the night before.
  • 🔒
    Athlone Lock — and you pass it twiceIt sits between Athlone town quay and the entrance to Lough Ree: northbound Saturday, southbound Sunday. Keeper-operated, no VHF, €1.50 by smart card or cash, allow 20–30 minutes. August hours 09:30–19:30 Mon–Sat, 09:30–18:00 Sunday, closed 13:00–14:00 for lunch. Gates open outside those hours only in exceptional circumstances — miss the last locking and you're on the wrong side of it overnight. Keeper: 090 649 2026.
  • ⛽
    HVO, water & gasSilver Spray departs with full tanks of all three. HVO used is paid at checkout (~8–10 L/hr at cruise for the Perkins 135). Water refills are free at marinas along the way; refuelling points are marked on the charts supplied.
  • 🔌
    Power & smart cards220V sockets and USB aboard. Shore power at Athlone quay runs off the smart card from Silverline's office — the same card does showers and lock fees. Run the engine at anchor to keep batteries topped.
  • 🚽
    Holding tankToilet waste goes to an onboard tank. Over four nights you likely won't need a pump-out, but stations are marked on the chart if the gauge says otherwise.
  • 🛒
    ProvisioningPre-order through Silverline and board a stocked boat, or shop in Banagher (5 min walk). Restock in Athlone on Day 4 — Shannonbridge has a village shop and not much more.
  • 🧭
    NavigationRed and green channel markers — keep red to starboard heading upstream. Five knots through the towns, wake discipline past moored boats. Charts and captain's handbook are supplied on board; this page is not a navigation aid.
  • 📶
    Signal & offlineFine in the towns, patchy on Lough Ree and at Killinure. Install this page to your home screen and it works offline — the live map and weather need signal, everything else doesn't. Download the Silverline PDFs and Clonmacnoise guide before you go.
  • 🛟
    Kids aboardLifejackets are supplied — on deck and on jetties, always. Engine off before anyone swims. Brief both on where to sit under way and to keep clear of whoever's on ropes during mooring.
  • 📋
    Deposit protectionPhoto/video walkthrough at handover and return, signed condition sheet both times. Deposit €800–€1,500 refundable, or the €200–€300 waiver (with €200 residual) on eligible boats. Know the carve-outs; check your travel policy for hire-boat excess cover.
