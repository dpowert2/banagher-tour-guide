Red to starboard heading upstream (north), green to port — reverse coming home. 5 knots through towns and past moored boats.

Distances are straight-line, so the river run will be 15–25% longer. ETA uses your actual speed when moving, 9 km/h when stopped.

This is not a chart. The track is an indicative line between moorings, not a surveyed channel. Steer by the Waterways Ireland chart aboard and by the markers on the water.

Schematic — not a survey

Distances and the order of features are real. Depths, buoy positions and arch clearances are not shown because I have no survey data for them — inventing them would be worse than leaving them out. For soundings and marks use the Waterways Ireland chart aboard, and your eyes.

What the chart can't tell you

Channel markers. Red to starboard going upstream (north), green to port. Coming home, reverse it. They are the actual navigation — the line on the map is not.

The Callows. Flat flood meadow either side from Banagher to Athlone. The river looks wide and is not; the navigable water is narrower than the water. Stay between the marks.

Lough Ree. Shoals run off the islands and off most points. Don't cut corners, hold the marked channel Athlone–Hodson Bay, and cross in the morning.

Bridge arches. Shannonbridge and Banagher both have one marked navigation arch. Line up early, go slow, and don't try to correct halfway through.

Athlone Lock

Checking…

The only lock on your route, and you pass it twice — north to Lough Ree on Saturday, south again on Sunday.

Aug hours: 09:30–19:30 Mon–Sat · 09:30–18:00 Sun · shut 13:00–14:00 for lunch. €1.50 by smart card or cash. Keeper-operated, no VHF. Gates open outside those hours only in exceptional circumstances.

Lock keeper Silverline

Sunday is the tight one

Coming south off Lough Ree the lock shuts at 18:00 and closes for lunch 13:00–14:00. Be through before one and the day stays simple. Miss the last locking and you're on the wrong side of it overnight.
